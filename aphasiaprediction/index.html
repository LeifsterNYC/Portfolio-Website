Broken speech,
restored.

Aphasia, often caused by stroke, can strip sentences down to fragments — the meaning intact, the grammar gone. This model reads the fragments and writes the sentence back.

or try:
restored

How it works

  1. 01

    Corrupt

    186,892 training pairs are made by breaking fluent everyday sentences the way aphasia does — dropped function words, fillers, repetitions, misplaced negation — while never deleting a “not” or a “he”, so meaning never inverts and nobody gets invented.

  2. 02

    Train

    A flan‑t5‑large sequence‑to‑sequence model is fine‑tuned to map each fragment back to its source sentence, with the best checkpoint selected by chrF on a leakage‑safe held‑out split, then quantized to 8‑bit to answer in about a third of a second on a CPU.

  3. 03

    Restore

    On 60 hand‑written test phrases the model scores chrF 83.5 and gets 43% word‑for‑word; the previous version scored 69.9 and invented a person the speaker never mentioned in 32% of them. This one does so in none.
